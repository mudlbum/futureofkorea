**Future of Korea** is an independent, English-language publication about where South Korea is heading — and what that means for people who invest in it, work in it, sell to it, or live in it.

## The problem we set out to fix

If you want to understand Korea in English, your options today are thin. Wire copy tells you what happened yesterday but not what it means. Paywalled terminals price out everyone who isn't an institution. Machine-translated aggregators are unreadable and frequently wrong. Meanwhile the specific, practical questions people actually type into a search box — *how do I buy Korean shares from abroad, what does the E-7 visa really require, why did the birth rate move, who actually supplies the AI memory chips* — are barely covered at all.

That gap is what this site is for. We write the explainer layer: durable, structured, sourced pieces that answer a question completely and stay accurate.

## What we cover

- **Markets & Investing** — KOSPI and KOSDAQ, ADRs and ETFs, the won, foreign-investor access, and the market-structure reforms that keep changing the rules.
- **Technology & Industry** — semiconductors, HBM and advanced packaging, EV batteries, shipbuilding, defence exports, robotics, and Korea's AI infrastructure build-out.
- **Living & Working in Korea** — visas, taxes, National Health Insurance, pensions, housing deposits, banking, and the paperwork nobody explains.
- **Demographics & Society** — fertility, ageing, regional depopulation, immigration policy, and the long-run arithmetic underneath every other story.
- **Policy & Geopolitics** — trade rules, industrial policy, regulation, the alliance, and North Korea risk as it actually affects markets.

## How we research

Every article is built from primary sources first: the Bank of Korea, Statistics Korea (KOSIS), the Korea Exchange, the Financial Supervisory Service, the Ministry of Economy and Finance, the Ministry of Justice's immigration portal, Korea Customs Service trade data, and company disclosures on DART. Secondary reporting is used to find stories, not to state facts.

We publish figures with the date they were current, link to the underlying source, and update pieces when the number moves. Where a claim is contested, we say so rather than picking a side.

## Authorship and AI

Articles are produced by the Future of Korea editorial desk using AI-assisted research and drafting tools, then fact-checked against primary sources and reviewed by a human editor before publication. We are explicit about this because you deserve to know how what you're reading was made. The full policy — including what AI is and is not allowed to do here — is on our [editorial &amp; AI policy](/editorial-policy/) page.

## How we're funded

Future of Korea is free to read and funded by advertising, served through Google AdSense. Advertisers have no input into what we cover or what we conclude, and no advertiser sees an article before it publishes. Ad units are labelled and kept out of the article body. Details are in our [privacy policy](/privacy-policy/) and [disclaimer](/disclaimer/).

## Corrections

We get things wrong sometimes. When we do, we fix the article, mark what changed, and say when. Report an error at [editor@futureofkorea.com](mailto:editor@futureofkorea.com) — see the [corrections policy](/corrections/).
